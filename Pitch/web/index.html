MOTTAINAI is a real-time cooperative mobile game: three players share one raft to clean a polluted river. Wajdi, show them how it plays. [HAND OVER TO WAJDI] [CLICK]

Both strokes within 0.25 s → 30 % more thrust
Three players, one raft. Two paddlers swipe to row, and the collector taps floating waste to throw a net. Here's the twist: the raft really flies when both paddlers stroke within a quarter of a second. That's the sync boost: thirty percent more thrust. Missing a friend? A bot takes the seat. [CLICK]

We balanced two worlds. On the fun side: combos, power-ups, sharks, and two-minute runs that make you want a rematch. On the learning side: every waste wears its real bin color, toxic waste costs you points, and every run ends with the kilos of river you cleaned. Kids play for the score, and leave with the habit. Our innovation
What makes MOTTAINAI different
01

Cooperation is the controller

Sync your strokes within 0.25 s and the raft surges +30 %. The raft needs the whole crew.

02

Real-time multiplayer on phones

One authoritative game room per match, 60 Hz, on Cloudflare's edge.

03

Built for 5G

Detects 5G SA / NSA and requests a low-latency network slice (Android 13+).

04

A network lab inside the game

Measures latency, jitter and bandwidth on every network, exported as CSV.

read · answer · forget→paddle · catch · remember
What makes MOTTAINAI different? One: cooperation is the controller: the raft needs the whole crew. Two: real-time multiplayer on phones, with a sixty-hertz game server on Cloudflare's edge. Three: it's built for 5G: the app detects 5G Standalone and asks the network for a low-latency slice. Four: a built-in network lab measures every connection. [CLICK] Learning stops being read, answer, forget. It becomes paddle, catch, remember. [CLICK]

And it's real engineering. Every match lives in one authoritative game room. Phones only send gestures; the server decides everything, so cheating is hard. We measured it: sixty updates per second, about two hundred kilobits per phone, forty-three to seventy-one milliseconds round trip, and sixty frames per second on our test phone.
